Most battery systems in the world are grid-following. They watch the grid's voltage and frequency and inject power to match. That works well when the grid is strong. When the grid is weak, or gone, they have nothing to follow.

## Leading, not following

A grid-forming system behaves like a spinning generator. It sets its own voltage and frequency and holds them. Loads connect to it the way they would connect to a turbine. If the wider grid falters, the station keeps the rhythm.

## Why it matters here

In a city where supply can drop at the busiest hour of the evening, following is not enough. The station has to hold the line, and then help bring the wider grid back. That is what our Core is built to do.

## The short version

- It holds frequency when the grid cannot.
- It restarts a dark network, one section at a time.
- It does all of this without waiting for an operator.
